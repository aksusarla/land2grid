About

Why land2grid exists.

Most renewable projects in India stall for the same three reasons: land that isn't clean, a grid connection that isn't confirmed, or a buyer that isn't locked in. land2grid exists because those three problems are usually handed to three different people, and the handoffs are where projects lose time.

Three workflows most teams underestimate. We don't.

Land acquisition, grid connectivity and industrial offtake get treated as paperwork to clear rather than the workflows that actually decide whether a project gets built. Each one is genuinely hard, each one has its own failure modes, and each one is where real projects quietly stall for months. We treat all three as core to the job, not side tasks to delegate, and we run them properly, with the technical and regulatory depth each one demands.

Our vision

We get into the workstreams, not just the slide deck.

land2grid exists because Rajib kept running into the same gap from inside the industry: developers and EPCs don't need another advisor pointing at the problem, they need someone willing to run the land diligence, file the connectivity application and sit across the table on the PPA. So that's the job we actually do, working the three problems ourselves, alongside the team building the project, instead of consulting from outside it.

The bigger goal behind that is simple. Every project we help get unstuck is one less gap in India's own power capacity. We want land2grid's work to be a small, repeatable part of how India gets to a cleaner, more energy self-sufficient grid, one project at a time.

How we think about the work.

One accountable owner

You deal with one person who owns land, connectivity and offtake together, not three vendors who each own a third of the problem and none of the outcome.

Sequenced, not siloed

Land, grid filings and customer outreach run in parallel on purpose, because each one changes what's realistic for the other two.

Built on real deal flow

Our process comes from having actually closed land, filed connectivity and negotiated PPAs, not from a generic project-management template.
