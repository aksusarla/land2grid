The integrated advantage

Why land, grid and offtake have to move together.

Land, grid connectivity and industrial offtake are usually handed to three different parties, and the handoffs between them are where projects lose months: land gets secured before anyone confirms the substation has headroom, or a PPA gets negotiated before connectivity is filed. Coordinating the three from day one removes that fragmentation, cuts execution friction, and reduces the commercial uncertainty that stalls a project between milestones.

Deal structures

Whatever structure the deal needs.

Open access

Third-party sale to industrial consumers under state open access rules, with wheeling, banking and cross-subsidy surcharge worked into the numbers before you commit.

Group captive

27%+ equity-linked captive structures for consumers who want a long-term hedge on power cost without taking on generation themselves.

Behind-the-meter (BTM)

On-site or near-site generation sized to a single consumer's load profile, where the connectivity and offtake conversations happen almost simultaneously.

Solar

Ground-mount utility-scale and C&I solar, from site screening through to commissioning support.

Wind and hybrid

Wind and wind-solar hybrid sites, where land and grid planning both have to account for a more demanding siting process.

Solar + storage

Projects pairing generation with BESS, where the grid connectivity filing and the offtake structure both change depending on how storage is sized.
