Case studies

How we'd structure a deal like yours.

These are illustrative scenarios built to show how land2grid approaches an engagement across land, grid and offtake together. They are not disclosures of specific client deals — once we have engagements we can name publicly, real ones will replace these.

Open access · Maharashtra

25 MW open access deal for a textile consumer

A mid-size textile manufacturer wanted to cut power cost without taking on generation risk directly.

  • Mapped wheeling, banking and cross-subsidy surcharge against actual monthly consumption data, not a generic assumption
  • Shortlisted generator-side land and connectivity in parallel with the consumer-side eligibility check
  • Structured the PPA tenor and tariff escalation around the consumer's own cost-of-power baseline
Group captive · Gujarat

40 MW captive structuring across multiple consumer sites

A multi-site FMCG consumer needed one captive structure that worked for plants with different load profiles.

  • Allocated equity participation and consumption share across sites rather than treating it as one uniform load
  • Sequenced land closure and connectivity filing so neither stalled waiting on the other
  • Negotiated banking terms that accounted for each site's seasonal consumption swing
Grid connectivity · Odisha

Evacuation planning for a 45 MW wind-solar hybrid site

A hybrid project needed a connectivity application that wouldn't stall on an assumed, rather than confirmed, substation capacity.

  • Verified actual substation headroom before filing, not the figure on a published capacity table
  • Planned the evacuation route jointly for the wind and solar components rather than as two separate filings
  • Tracked the application through utility queries to final approval and charging
Behind-the-meter · Telangana

15 MW BTM solar sized to a food-processing consumer's load

An on-site project only works if the generation is sized to how the consumer actually draws power through the day.

  • Built the generation sizing around the consumer's hourly load curve, not its nameplate demand
  • Coordinated land and rooftop/ground-mount feasibility alongside the consumer's own site constraints
  • Structured the offtake agreement around the consumer's existing tariff category
